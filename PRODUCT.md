## Platform

web

## Users

Two audiences, weighted equally:

- **Nepal trek and tour agencies** (Kathmandu/Pokhara-style operators selling treks, tours and expeditions). Served by `/nepal` with NPR pricing.
- **Small to mid-size tour and trekking operators worldwide**. Served by the homepage and `/pricing` with USD pricing.

The buyer is the agency owner or manager. They are not a developer. Their job is to get a professional website and trip catalog online, keep itineraries and pricing current, and turn website visits into trip inquiries and bookings without hiring developers.

## Product Purpose

TripEleven lets an agency build, manage and sell trips online from one dashboard:

- build structured trips
- publish a branded website from that catalog
- collect inquiries and bookings

Success means the agency is live on its own domain within days, keeps its catalog current without help, and receives inquiries and bookings directly.

## Positioning

TripEleven is built specifically for tour and trekking agencies, not adapted from a generic site builder. The trip is the core object:

- itineraries have day-by-day structure, including altitude and trip facts
- pricing and FAQs sit on the trip
- the website, catalog and inquiry flow all derive from those structured trips

## Operating Context

- Agencies manage a catalog of packages. Each package has an itinerary, trip facts, pricing and FAQs.
- They publish to a branded website on their own custom domain.
- Inquiries arrive by email or WhatsApp. Bookings and payments go through the agency's own Stripe keys.
- Small teams work together through staff seats.
- Prospects evaluate by visiting live client sites, starting a 7-day free trial at `app.tripeleven.com/signup`, or contacting the team.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Confirmed capabilities, from site copy and plans:

- Trip builder: guided workflow for itineraries, trip facts, pricing and FAQs.
- AI itinerary drafts: bring your own key; drafts need review before publishing.
- Branded website and catalog: custom domain, template, palette and fonts.
- Content publishing: blog posts.
- Built-in SEO toolkit, including handling of slug changes.
- Trip inquiries (email / WhatsApp), direct bookings and payments via own Stripe keys.
- Media library (search by filename, alt text, caption).
- Info pages: static pages such as visa guides, festivals, activities, about/contact, with categories and drafts.
- Blog posts with categories and authors.
- Organization: departments, team members (public team profiles), authors, staff users.
- Navigation builder: auto nav generated from site content, or a manual nested menu builder. Call it "navigation", not "menus", in marketing copy so it is never read as a restaurant-style menu.
- Team seats.
- Read-only API (Business).
- White-label (Enterprise).

## Product Principles

1. **Speak operator.** Use the agency's own vocabulary: itineraries, departures, trip facts, packages, inquiries, bookings. Never use generic SaaS or other-industry language.
2. **Real proof only.** Live client sites are the evidence. Never imply demos, videos, metrics or testimonials that don't exist.
3. **The trip is the product.** Lead with the structured trip and what it powers (website, catalog, inquiries), not with a list of modules.
4. **No developers needed.** Every claim and flow should reinforce that a non-technical owner can run it alone.
5. **Two markets, one truth.** Nepal and global pages can differ in pricing and emphasis, but must never contradict each other on capabilities.
